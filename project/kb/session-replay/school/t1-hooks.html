When to look: hooks

A recorder has to decide when to look at the screen. The fundamentals showed what each platform offers; this lesson shows what vendors actually hook, and plays the same 30 seconds of a session through five strategies.

Builds on: The event loop and frames · View trees, platform by platform · Keyframes and deltas

Every recorder answers three questions about timing:

  1. Keyframe or delta? When do you write the whole screen again, and when only what changed?
  2. What wakes the recorder? A change notification, a timer, a frame callback, a user event, a lifecycle event.
  3. When is a capture not worth sending? Unchanged frames, throttles, idle back-off.

The fourth, when to send, is lesson When to send.

The shape to remember: a change signal decides whether there is anything to capture, a clock decides how often at most, a hash decides whether it is worth sending, and the lifecycle decides when it must go out. The web gets the first for free (MutationObserver). Mobile SDKs approximate it with run-loop observers, draw listeners, swizzled layers or post-frame callbacks, then cap it with a clock.
