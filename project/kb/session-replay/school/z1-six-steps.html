Six steps

Strip the vendor names and every recorder in the technique lessons is the same six steps. Only two are real design questions, what to record and how to mask; the rest are plumbing or optimisation.

1 · Hookwhen to look
2 · Recordpixels or structure
3 · Maskhide what is private
4 · Packimages, tiles, video, JSON
5 · Shipouo.replay.chunk
6 · Playa player per format
  • Hook is a callback or loop you register: MutationObserver on the web, a run-loop observer on iOS, a draw listener plus a 1 s timer on Android, a post-frame callback in Flutter (hooks).
  • Record has only two families. Pixels: take a frame. Structure: describe what is on screen. Mobile wireframes and the web's DOM snapshots are the same family; draw commands are structure too, recorded one level lower.
  • Mask is where the two families really differ. With structure, masking is a data transform (write ••• instead of the text) and cannot miss. With pixels, masking is geometry: boxes painted on a picture that was taken at a slightly different moment from the tree walk, so a moving view can slip out (the masking race).
  • Pack is optimisation only. JPEG, 64 px tiles, WebP or H.264 change bytes and CPU, never what the replay shows or whether masking is right. "Tiles vs video" is not a design question.
  • Ship is ours and stays ours: an OTLP log record through the OpenTelemetry SDK (when to send).
  • Play is the hidden cost: every format we record needs a player in the Grafana plugin.

Order: start with the format we can already play

  1. Sentry video (iOS, then Android). The plugin already plays kind: "video" from the Sentry bridge (ADR-0008, video.ts), so the first borrowed recorder needs almost no player work. A spike session is porting the iOS core now.
  2. PostHog wireframes. PostHog writes mobile screens as rrweb-shaped events, close to the web player ouo already has. Not checked in the player code yet.
  3. Datadog wireframes last. Their player was not found in the open; assume ouo writes the renderer.

Each chunk names its format in ouo.replay.format (for example sentry-video, later posthog-wireframe), so one SDK can carry several recorders and the reader picks a decoder per chunk, as the draft spec already allows (choice 7 of the open choices).

What borrowing Sentry's recorder gives up, measured on the Step 5 light journey: a static screen no longer costs zero bytes (Sentry sends a full picture at the start of every 5 s segment), and the drop-if-moving mask check is gone. Bytes: Sentry iOS 49.5 KiB/min vs ouo 38.8; Android about 193 vs 142 (overhead.md). The drop-if-moving check can be kept as a small patch on the borrowed code if the masking experiment shows Sentry's masking leaks.

The founder's five cases

Your five cases
  1. One frame a second, masked reliably. The open question; the experiment compares drop-if-moving, walk-after-copy, presentation layers, and two box fixes (union of before/after walks, boxes grown by speed).
  2. Raise the rate when something changes? No pixel recorder does it today. Datadog iOS captures structure up to every 100 ms when layers change, which is cheap because wireframes are small. For pixels, a higher rate costs main-thread time per capture, not mainly bytes. Worth a later experiment within ouo's 100 ms/s time budget.
  3. Several still frames. Already solved by packing: ouo sends nothing; video sends skip blocks.
  4. Only a field, a button or a spinner changes. Also solved by packing: tiles send one tile, video sends a few blocks. Leave it.
  5. When to send, bandwidth or phone compute? Time (5 s), size (256 KiB target), lifecycle (background), and a disk queue for retries. The expensive part on the phone is capture and masking on the main thread, not packing; the spike measures it.
