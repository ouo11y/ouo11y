How to play

A recording is only useful with a player that can rebuild it. For the web that means rebuilding a real DOM, safely, on a clock, with fast seeking. For every other format it means a renderer of its own, which is the hidden cost of supporting several formats.

Builds on: Keyframes and deltas · What to record

The rrweb player, step by step

The player must never run the user's code. rrweb rebuilds the page in an iframe sandboxed to exactly allow-same-origin and turns recorded <script> tags into <noscript>. And seeking is only fast because many deltas are applied to a virtual DOM and patched into the real one in one diff, the player-side answer to keyframes and deltas.

"My app moves much faster than once a second, yet Sentry replays look acceptable."

They are 1 fps. Two things hide it (reasoning, not measured): most app time is spent on still screens, where 1 fps loses nothing; and the player draws touches and navigation breadcrumbs from their own timestamps, so taps and screen changes look precise even though the pixels between them are one second apart. A fling or an animation does look like a jump.
