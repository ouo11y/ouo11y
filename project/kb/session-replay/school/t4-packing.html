How to pack

Packing turns captures into bytes: JSON and gzip for structure, JPEG, tiles, WebP or H.264 for pixels. It is pure optimisation. This lesson maps every technique onto bytes and answers the founder's packing questions from the code.

Builds on: Compression basics, and how H.264 skips · What to record

Pack is optimisation only. JPEG, 64 px tiles, WebP or H.264 change bytes and CPU, never what the replay shows or whether masking is right. "Tiles vs video" is not a design question.

ouo tiles versus Sentry video

ouo also captures about once a second. What is the difference from Sentry?

The hook and the rate are the same idea. The differences are in mask and pack:

ouo mobile todaySentry
Static screennothing sent: tiles are hashed, no change means no bytesa frame every second anyway: Android re-sends the last bitmap, iOS repeats it inside the video
Small change (spinner, one field)only the changed 64 px tiles, as JPEG patchesthe codec sends only the changed blocks
Scrollevery tile changes, so a full JPEGvideo wins: the codec says "these blocks moved up 12 px"
Masked view movingframe dropped (fails closed)kept: Android walks the tree after the copy, iOS reads the animation layers' live positions

Why does H.264 encode an unchanged frame almost for free?

The encoder cuts each frame into 16×16-pixel blocks. In a P-frame (a frame stored as changes from the one before), a block that matches the previous frame is marked skip: about one bit, no pixels. A block that moved costs a short motion vector plus a small correction. So an unchanged frame is a few dozen bytes. The catch: Sentry iOS forces a full picture (key frame) at least every 6 frames (MaxKeyFrameInterval = 6), and every 5 s segment is its own MP4 that starts with one. So a static screen still costs one full picture per segment; Step 6 measured about 36 KB per idle 5 s segment on Android.
