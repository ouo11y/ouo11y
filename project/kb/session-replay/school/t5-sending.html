When to send, and how ouo ships

Capturing and sending are separate decisions. This lesson covers the four triggers for sending, why the page-close moment is the one that matters, and how any technique leaves the device as an ouo.replay.chunk under the draft spec.

Builds on: Keyframes and deltas · How to pack

Four triggers

Capturing and sending are separate decisions. Every SDK keeps captured data in a buffer and uploads it on one of four triggers. Time: Sentry web 5 s after the last event (at most 5.5 s), Datadog 5 s, PostHog 2 s, ouo mobile 5 s. Size: Datadog 60,000 compressed bytes, under the browser's 64 KB unload budget; PostHog about 0.9 MB; ouo 1 MiB of images. Lifecycle: tab hidden, page hide, app background. An error: in buffer mode Sentry keeps 30 to 60 s in memory and sends it only when an error happens. Lifecycle is the trigger that matters most, because it is the last moment the code is guaranteed to run. On the web after visibilitychange → hidden the tab may be killed without another event; on iOS, after didEnterBackground the app may be suspended within seconds.

One limit cuts across options 2 and 3: at page unload all keepalive requests share 64 KiB, and the draft spec keeps them under 60 KiB with telemetry first (TRN-16). A 256 KiB chunk cannot leave during unload; it stays persisted and a later page sends it (TRN-18). Datadog caps segments at 60,000 compressed bytes for the same reason (Datadog on the web).

When does ouo export frames?

A chunk closes at the first of: 5 s, 1 MiB of images, pause or background, a session rotation, a screen-size change. It is written to disk, then sent as its own request with retries. A chunk with no frames and no touches is not sent. The draft spec keeps time and lifecycle and lowers the size target to 256 KiB (hard limit 1 MiB).

How ouo would ship it: every technique as an ouo.replay.chunk

The working assumption: whatever the capture technique, its output leaves the device as ouo.replay.chunk records, with the payload either in the record's body or held elsewhere and referenced. This topic shows what the repository already says, maps each technique from topics 2 to 5 onto that record, and lays out the choices still open. Evidence only: nothing here decides the format.

Two layers in the repo: what runs today, and the draft spec

Today (MVP-era code)
  • The SDK posts a gzip JSON chunk to POST /v1/replay/chunks on the Collector's ouoreplay receiver (port 4319): config.go.
  • Three kinds: rrweb (events), frames (JPEG full images and tile patches, base64 in the JSON: mobile-replay spec) and video (MP4 base64, made by the Sentry bridge from Sentry's replay_video: sentry-bridge spec §9–11).
  • The receiver validates the chunk and wraps the whole JSON as the bytes body of one log record, with session.id, ouo.replay.seq, ouo.replay.kind and ouo.replay.encoding = json: receiver.go (buildLogs).
  • The ouoreplay exporter posts the body on to rum-api, which stores one file per chunk; a session cannot mix kinds (409).
Draft spec (post-MVP Steps 2 and 3)
  • A chunk is an OTLP log record, event ouo.replay.chunk, scope ouo.replay, sent over OTLP/HTTP protobuf so bytes are not base64'd: sdk-spec.md §11, TRN-1.
  • Hierarchy session → replay → stream → segment → chunk; a segment starts with a keyframe and is decodable alone (RPL-6); each chunk body is independently decompressible, and a segment's bodies concatenated are a valid payload of ouo.replay.format (RPL-7).
  • Chunks close at 256 KiB compressed (hard limit 1 MiB), 5 s, stream end, or hide/background (RPL-10); a keyframe at least every 5 min and on visibility change (RPL-9).
  • Bytes to the Collector, then the Collector stores them and replaces the body with ouo.replay.ref (decision D3, recommended and applied, not yet decided): replay.md.
  • The format is pluggable and the mobile format is open (§11.6).

What survives every option: the chunk record itself. Identity (session.id, replay, stream, sequence), the keyframe flag, the format and encoding labels and the hash are the same whether the body is rrweb JSON, wireframe records, JPEG tiles or an MP4. That is exactly what RPL-23 asks for ("the hierarchy, identity, chunk attributes, delivery and end rules MUST NOT depend on the format"), and it is why the mobile format can stay open while the transport is built.
